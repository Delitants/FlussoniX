# Encoded worker output decoder foundation

`worker_output::Decoder` reconstructs native `Track`/`Frame` events from independently produced MPEG-TS. The [live worker integration](worker-ts-live.md) now connects this decoder to the native hub and replaces the active FLV bridge. The decoder foundation itself adds no encoder setting.

The initial profile covers one stable program, up to 16 AV tracks and one video: H.264, single-layer HEVC, AAC-LC in unprotected ADTS, MPEG Layer II and MPEG Layer III. Video PES packets must start their coded picture before any continuation slice and contain at most one coded picture and a stable parameter-set family. The decoder derives AVC/hvcC initialization, retains picture/SEI bytes, removes ADTS headers, and preserves complete MPEG audio frames. It waits for all track initialization before emitting metadata and samples. IDs are transport PIDs; consumers must start a fresh hub generation rather than equate them with original input IDs.

Call `push` with arbitrary boundaries and at most 12032 bytes per call, collect its events, then call `finish` on clean EOF to flush the final unbounded-length video PES. `Info` precedes all frames. Frames follow PES completion order, which can differ from global DTS order; each track is monotonic. A remuxer requiring global order must reorder with a bounded interleave queue. For live publication, the worker owner must handle generation changes, incomplete EOF/errors and authorization separately. Any decoder error is terminal, and further calls fail.

The decoder expands shared 33-bit clocks across wrap and preserves signed video composition offsets. If the first completed track starts just after wrap and another first track starts just before it, the decoder resolves and retimes the common epoch while metadata and frames are still unpublished. Published generations cannot be retimed. AAC/MPEG frame clocks use cumulative sample counts. A PES PTS anchors its first newly starting audio frame; a continuation of a frame started in the previous PES retains that frame's saved clock. Integral PES timestamps within one tick of the predicted next audio clock preserve its fractional remainder. A nondecreasing PES clock may still place the next complete audio frame inside the preceding frame's decoded sample span. For overlaps of at most 250 ms, native reconstruction retains the cumulative sample clock and original compressed bytes instead of compressing encoded duration. Forward gaps are preserved; overlap beyond 250 ms closes the generation rather than accumulating unlimited audio/video drift. Audio PTS must equal DTS. Clock jumps larger than 60 seconds or backwards source PES DTS require a new decoder generation. This correction applies to reconstructed native media, including its RTP/SRTP packetization; it does not rewrite FFmpeg's original live MPEG-TS bytes.

Limits include 16 MiB per PES/sample, 32 MiB retained media including waiting frame overhead, 65536 waiting samples, 64 KiB initialization and 4096 NALs per access unit. PAT/PMT require valid CRC, current single-section tables and one program. Changed layouts/parameter sets, gaps/conflicting duplicates, scrambling, discontinuity indicators, unsupported stream types, protected/multiple-block ADTS and incomplete final data fail explicitly. Exact packet duplicates are ignored. This validates framing and initialization fields, not the complete compressed-picture/audio syntax; independent decoders qualify the owned codec profiles.

Qualification includes bytewise input, MPEG identity, split/aggregated audio, fractional clocks, signed offsets and wrap, initialization ordering/limits, malformed transport/PES/audio and independent FFmpeg-generated AVC/AAC, HEVC/Layer II and Main10 HEVC/Layer III. Each independently generated video profile must reconstruct all 25 pictures, and independent decoded picture/audio sample hashes must match the original transport.
